Abstract

Second-hand e-commerce marketplaces face a hybrid threat landscape in which every major attack simultaneously leaves evidence at both the network and application layers. Conventional IDS systems, operating at a single layer, are structurally insufficient.

This work presents a systematic comparative study of 24 IDS methods across four generations (1994–2026), evaluated on six performance dimensions. Three structural gaps are identified and addressed through a novel multi-modal cross-layer architecture validated with real Kali Linux attack campaigns.

4
Three Research Gaps
Gap 1 — Network-Application Divide
All 24 reviewed systems operate at a single layer. Cross-layer behavioral correlation is entirely absent from the published literature.
Gap 2 — Lack of Domain Specificity
Generic benchmarks do not capture marketplace behavioral patterns (rating manipulation, catalog scraping, coordinated fake accounts).
Gap 3 — Opaque Model Outputs
Only 2 of 24 reviewed methods provide full per-alert explainability. Analysts cannot act reliably on unexplained probability scores.

10
Conclusion
This is the first IDS system simultaneously addressing cross-layer correlation, marketplace domain specificity, and full SHAP explainability. The comparative study confirms that F1 performance has plateaued at 0.97–0.98 in Generation 4. The remaining frontier is architectural: no published system fuses network and application behavioral signals for a marketplace threat model. SoukDZ fills this gap with a deployable, explainable, production-ready architecture.
